If you're holding a raw card and wondering what PSA will charge to slab it, here's the short answer: **$59.99 per card minimum** — and the real all-in cost is closer to $85 once shipping and supplies are counted. The cheap Value tiers that used to make grading a $25 decision were paused in June 2026, and they haven't come back.

This guide breaks down every open tier, the hidden fees nobody mentions, and the math that tells you whether your card is even worth submitting.

## The 2026 PSA tier list

PSA prices per card based on two things: how fast you want it back, and the declared value of the card. Each tier caps the maximum declared value — submit a card worth more than the cap and PSA bumps you to the pricier tier automatically.

| Tier | Fee per card | Max declared value | Turnaround |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | $59.99 | $1,000 | 90–100 business days |
| Priority | $79.99 | $1,500 | 70–80 business days |
| Express | $199 | $2,500 | 20–30 business days |
| Super Express | $349 | $5,000 | 10–15 business days |
| Premier | $599 | $10,000 | 7–10 business days |
| Premium 1 | $999 | $25,000 | 5–7 business days |

Above Premium 1, tiers keep climbing for cards declared over $25,000 — priced as a percentage of value, up to $9,999+ per card for quarter-million-dollar pieces.

**What's missing:** the Value Bulk ($24.99), Value ($32.99), Value Plus ($49.99) and Value Max ($64.99) tiers. All four were paused on June 2, 2026 when PSA's backlog exploded past 10 million cards. In September 2026 PSA introduced the new $59.99 Standard tier as partial relief — the first price break collectors had seen in months — but the sub-$50 era of grading is, for now, over.

## The hidden costs nobody puts in the headline

The tier fee is maybe two-thirds of what you'll actually spend. Here's a realistic 20-card Standard submission:

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Grading fees (20 × $59.99) | $1,200 |
| Shipping to PSA | ~$20 |
| Return shipping + insurance | ~$20–40 |
| Card Savers, team bags, box | ~$15 |
| **Total** | **~$1,255** |
| **Effective per card** | **~$63–85** |

Wait — why did I say $85 earlier? Because most collectors don't submit 20 cards at once. On a 5-card submission, fixed shipping and supply costs spread across fewer cards, pushing the effective cost toward $80–90 per card. Solo submitters feel this the most.

Then there are **upcharges**: if PSA determines your card is worth more than the tier's max declared value, they move it up a tier and bill you the difference. Declare honestly — PSA checks values at intake.

## The only math that matters

Grading is worth it when:

**PSA 10 sale price − raw sale price − all-in cost = profit**

And the profit needs to be big enough to justify locking your card (and your money) up for 3–5 months. A common rule of thumb from experienced submitters: don't grade anything where the expected upside at PSA 10 is under $100, and don't grade raw cards worth under ~$40 — at $59.99+ a slab, the math almost never works below that line.

Example — a card selling raw at $90, with recent PSA 10 sales at $720:

- PSA 10 price: $720
- Raw price: $90
- All-in cost: ~$85
- **Profit if PSA 10: $545** → worth submitting

But notice the trap: if that same card comes back a PSA 9 and 9s sell for $155, you've *lost* money ($155 − $90 − $85 = −$20). The grade you *expect* matters as much as the prices. Be brutally honest about centering, corners, edges and surface before you submit.

## How to pay less per card

1. **Submit in bulk.** Fixed shipping costs spread thinner across 20+ cards.
2. **Use a middleman.** Grading middlemen consolidate submissions; their per-card rates often beat solo Standard submissions once you factor shipping both ways.
3. **Pick the cheapest tier your card qualifies for.** Don't pay Express ($199) for impatience unless the card's price is time-sensitive — most cards aren't.
4. **Skip grading entirely when the math fails.** The best money in grading is the submission you didn't make. Run the numbers first — that's literally why WorthASlab exists.

## The 2026 reality check

PSA graded a record 2.5 million cards in June 2026, and 71% of them were trading card games — Pokémon and friends are the engine of the grading economy now, up 95% year over year. Demand isn't cooling. With Value tiers paused, every submission decision carries more weight than it did two years ago.

So before you fill out that submission form, do the five-second check: raw price, PSA 10 price, all-in cost. If the upside isn't there, keep it raw — or sell it to someone whose math works better than yours.

## PSA grading cost vs the other graders

PSA isn't the only slab in town, and in 2026 the fee gap matters more than ever:

| Grader | Entry-level fee (2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PSA | $59.99 (Standard) | Highest resale premiums in most categories |
| CGC | Lower tiers available | Growing fast; credible PSA alternative, smaller premiums |
| SGC | Competitive | Loyal vintage-sports niche |
| BGS/Beckett | Varies | Premium vintage + autographs |

The decision is always net, not gross: (sale price at that grader) minus (all-in cost at that grader). A $55 CGC submission that nets $365 can beat an $85 PSA submission that nets $380 on thin-margin cards — but PSA's deeper buyer pool usually wins on anything with real demand. Run both numbers; don't grade on brand loyalty.

## 2026 price outlook: will it get cheaper?

Two forces are pulling in opposite directions. Downward pressure: PSA's backlog is falling from its ~14M peak toward ~10M, throughput is up (five grading hubs), and the September 2026 Standard tier at $59.99 was an explicit price concession. If the backlog keeps dropping toward ~5M, PSA has hinted Value-style tiers could return.

Upward pressure: demand keeps growing — TCG submissions up 95% year over year, a record 2.5M cards in a single month. Every price cut gets instantly absorbed by new submitters.

Our read: don't plan around cheaper grading. The $59.99 floor is the reality for the foreseeable future, and every submission decision should be profitable *at today's prices*. If Value tiers return, that's upside — not something to wait for while your cards sit raw. The collectors who win in 2026 are the ones whose math works at $85 all-in, because that's the world we're actually in.
