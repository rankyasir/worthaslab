Time is the hidden fee of grading. Everyone budgets the $59.99; nobody budgets the five months. In 2026, with PSA's backlog swinging between 10 and 14 million cards, turnaround times aren't just an inconvenience — they're a variable in your ROI math.

## Current turnarounds by tier (2026)

| Tier | Fee | Published turnaround | In plain English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | $59.99 | 90–100 business days | 4.5–5 months |
| Priority | $79.99 | 70–80 business days | 3.5–4 months |
| Express | $199 | 20–30 business days | 1–1.5 months |
| Super Express | $349 | 10–15 business days | 2–3 weeks |
| Premier | $599 | 7–10 business days | ~2 weeks |
| Premium 1 | $999 | 5–7 business days | ~1 week |

"Business days" is doing heavy lifting in that table — 100 business days is 20 weeks, roughly five calendar months. Add 1–2 weeks each way for shipping and intake logging, and a Standard submission can easily span half a year door to door.

## Why the backlog exploded

PSA graded a record 2.5 million cards in a single month in mid-2026 — and still couldn't keep up, because submissions grew faster. Two forces collided:

1. **The TCG wave.** Trading card games (Pokémon et al.) now drive 71% of PSA's volume, up 95% year over year. Millions of new collectors discovered grading at once.
2. **The Value-tier pause.** When PSA paused the $24.99–$64.99 Value tiers in June 2026, a flood of submissions rushed in before the cutoff — then the cheaper backlog had to be worked through with fewer intake paths.

PSA has been investing heavily (five grading hubs, nine-figure infrastructure spend) and the backlog has started falling — from ~14 million at peak toward ~10 million. The new $59.99 Standard tier, launched September 2026, was only possible because throughput improved. But "improved" still means months, not weeks, at the cheap tiers.

## How wait times change your math

A five-month wait isn't free. It costs you in three ways:

**1. Capital lock-up.** Your card + $85 are frozen. If you're a flipper, that's inventory you can't redeploy. Price this like any locked capital — would you tie up $500 for five months for a $150 profit? Sometimes yes, often no.

**2. Price decay.** Modern cards depreciate. A card worth $1,000 at PSA 10 today might be $800 when it pops in March — set hype fades, new sets release, metas shift. Vintage is more stable; modern is perishable. If your card decays ~5% monthly, five months eats 25% of the upside. Run the math on the *future* price, not today's.

**3. Opportunity cost of the tier decision.** Express ($199) buys back ~70 business days vs Standard. That's $139 for ~3.5 months. Worth it only if the card's decay or your capital velocity exceeds $40/month. For most holds, it doesn't — patience is the highest-ROI "tier" in grading.

## Strategy by collector type

**The holder** (grading for the PC or long-term): Standard tier, always. Time doesn't matter if you're never selling. Don't pay for speed you'll never use.

**The flipper** (grading to sell): model the decay. If the card is time-sensitive — playoff hype, set release window, movie tie-in — Express can be the difference between selling the top and selling the comedown. Otherwise Standard, and price your flips on conservative future comps.

**The investor** (grading as an asset play): turnaround is irrelevant; pop report and grade probability are everything. But note — submit during *low* backlog periods if you can time it. PSA's published estimates lag reality; watch collector forums for real-world return reports.

## Tracking your submission

PSA's online submission tracker shows intake → grading → encapsulation → shipping stages. Realistic expectations: "intake" alone can take 2–4 weeks at Standard during busy periods — your cards sitting in a warehouse queue, not yet being graded. Don't panic; that's normal now.

## The bottom line

In 2026, grading is a slow game at the tiers most collectors use. Budget five months, model price decay for modern cards, and never pay Express for impatience alone. The cheapest tier plus patience remains the highest expected-value play in the hobby — which is exactly why the WorthASlab calculator defaults to Standard and shows you the time-adjusted math.

## How to track your submission

Once PSA has your cards, you're in the system. Practical tracking advice:

- **Log your submission the day you ship.** Order number, card list, tier, date shipped. You'll thank yourself in month four when you're trying to remember what's where.
- **Understand the stages.** PSA's tracker moves through: Order Received → Intake/Logging → Grading → Encapsulation → QA → Shipping. "Intake" is the black hole — cards can sit in receiving for weeks during busy periods before grading even starts. This is normal, not a lost package.
- **Don't email them at week six.** Standard tier at 90–100 business days means exactly that. Status inquiries don't speed anything up. Set a calendar reminder for the published estimate *plus* a month, and only then start wondering.
- **Watch the community, not just the tracker.** Collector forums and Discords are full of real-world return reports — "my Standard order from March just popped" tells you more about actual throughput than any published estimate.

## Expedited alternatives (and when they're worth it)

Sometimes waiting isn't an option. Your options, honestly assessed:

- **Higher PSA tier.** Express at $199 buys ~70 business days vs Standard. Worth it when the card's price decay or your capital velocity exceeds ~$40/month. For a $5,000 card, it's obviously fine. For a $300 card, it's usually ego.
- **Grading middlemen with bulk slots.** Some middlemen run monthly bulk submissions at various tiers — occasionally faster effective turnaround than solo Standard, sometimes cheaper all-in. Vet them carefully: insurance, reputation, years in business.
- **A different grader.** CGC and SGC turnarounds are often shorter than PSA's at comparable tiers. If the card's resale works at that grader's premiums (run the math!), faster can beat "best."
- **Buy already graded.** The ultimate expedite: someone else waited five months and you buy the slab today. Compare (graded price) vs (raw + grading + waiting) — frequently the graded copy is cheaper than the DIY route once you count everything. The market often misprices patience.

Time is a cost. Price it like one, and the "slow" tier usually wins — but now you'll know exactly when it doesn't.
