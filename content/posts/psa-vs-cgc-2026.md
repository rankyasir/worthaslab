For a decade the answer was simple: PSA, no contest. In 2026 it's still the default — but for the first time, there's a real conversation. CGC is growing explosively, collectors are openly asking for a PSA alternative, and the duopoly the hobby wanted is forming. Here's the honest breakdown.

## Where things stand

**PSA** remains the market leader by an enormous margin: record 2.5-million-card months, the deepest buyer pool, and the highest resale premiums in most categories. When someone says "slabbed," they picture a PSA slab. That brand power translates directly into dollars — PSA 10s routinely outsell equivalent grades from every competitor.

**CGC** (backed by Blackstone, via Certified Collectibles Group) is the challenger with momentum: triple-digit growth, increasingly respected grading standards, and a collector base that's actively rooting for it. The "we want an alternative to PSA" sentiment is real and measurable.

**BGS/Beckett** remains relevant for premium vintage and autographs but has lost share. **SGC** holds a loyal niche, particularly in vintage sports.

## The comparison that actually matters: resale

Forget slab aesthetics and turnaround marketing. The only question that matters for most submitters: **how much more does the graded card sell for, and what did the grading cost?**

The pattern in 2026:

- **PSA 10 vs CGC 10 (same card):** PSA wins on price in the large majority of cases, often by 20–50%+. The gap is widest in Pokémon and modern sports — narrowest in some TCG niches where CGC has loyal buyers.
- **PSA 9 vs CGC 9:** smaller gap, but PSA still ahead on liquidity — more buyers, faster sales.
- **Fees:** CGC's pricing has historically undercut PSA's, which matters enormously now that PSA's floor is $59.99. A cheaper slab changes the break-even math for mid-value cards.

So the decision isn't "which grader is better" — it's arithmetic per card:

**(Grader A sale price − Grader A cost) vs (Grader B sale price − Grader B cost)**

A card where PSA 10 sells for $500 and CGC 10 sells for $420, with PSA costing $85 all-in and CGC costing $55 all-in: PSA nets $415, CGC nets $365. PSA still wins — but the gap is $50, not $80. And for cards where the PSA premium is thin, the cheaper CGC submission can win outright.

## When to choose PSA

- **Maximizing resale value** is the goal (flips, investments)
- The card is **Pokémon or mainstream sports** — PSA's premium is largest here
- You want the **deepest buyer pool** and fastest liquidity
- Registry-set collectors are your buyers (PSA's registry drives demand)

## When to consider CGC

- The card is for your **personal collection** — you keep the grading quality, skip the PSA premium you don't need
- **Cost matters more than max resale** — mid-value cards where PSA's $59.99+ floor breaks the math but a cheaper tier works
- You believe in the **convergence thesis** — that CGC premiums will keep rising as the duopoly matures, making today's CGC 10s tomorrow's bargains
- **Turnaround** — when PSA's backlog stretches to 5 months, a faster alternative has real value for time-sensitive cards

## The strategic take

Smart collectors in 2026 aren't monogamous about graders. The playbook:

1. **Run the math for both.** Same card, both companies' fees, both companies' realized prices. Let arithmetic pick.
2. **PSA for the money cards.** Your five-figure grails and prime flips go PSA — the premium dwarfs the fee difference.
3. **CGC for the PC and the margins.** Personal collection? CGC. Mid-value card where PSA's floor kills the deal? CGC.
4. **Watch the trend, don't marry it.** If CGC premiums keep converging, today's calculus changes. Revisit yearly.

## The bottom line

PSA is still the king of resale in 2026, and for profit-driven submissions it's still the default answer. But "default" isn't "always" — CGC's rise has created genuine optionality, especially for collectors who grade for love rather than money, or whose cards sit in the awkward middle where PSA's $59.99 floor is the whole problem.

## The data behind the premiums

Let's be concrete about what "PSA premium" means in practice, using the patterns visible across 2026 sold data:

- **Pokémon alt arts:** PSA 10s routinely command 30–60% over CGC 10s on the same card. The gap is the market's vote, repeated thousands of times a month.
- **Modern sports rookies:** PSA leads by 20–40% at gem mint. The gap narrows at 9 and below — liquidity, not just price, is PSA's edge here.
- **Vintage (pre-1980):** PSA's dominance is most entrenched — registry culture is strongest in vintage, and the premium reflects decades of buyer habit.
- **Niche TCGs:** the smallest gaps in the hobby. Where buyer pools are thin, the PSA brand matters less and the grade itself matters more.

These are directional, not gospel — individual cards vary, and the trend is what matters: **CGC's discount to PSA has been shrinking, not growing.** That's the single most important chart in grading right now.

## What would change our mind

Intellectual honesty requires stating the falsification conditions. We'd revise this guide's default-to-PSA stance if:

1. **CGC 10 premiums reach parity** on high-volume cards for 3+ consecutive quarters — not a spike, a trend.
2. **PSA's fee floor rises again** while competitors hold — every $10 PSA adds shifts more mid-value cards into CGC's winning zone.
3. **Liquidity flips in a category** — if CGC 10s start selling faster than PSA 10s somewhere meaningful, that's a regime change.

Until then: PSA for the money, CGC for the smart margins and the PC, math for everything. And whichever slab you choose — the five-second calculator check before you submit. That part never changes.
